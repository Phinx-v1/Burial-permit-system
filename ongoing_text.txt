Dec 202 6 ABSTRACT This study presents the development of  a web-based burial permit processing and geomapped cemetery information system for the Municipal Civic Registrar Office of LGU Carmen. The current manual system, which relies on paper records and Excel files, results in data redundancy, difficulty in locating burial plots, and inefficient record management. The proposed system integrates a centralized SQL database with geomapping technology to improve data accuracy, accessibility, and decision-making. The system features burial permit processing, cemetery mapping, duplicate detection, and reporting tools. The Agile development methodology was used in system development, with iterative testing and user feedback. Results show that the system improves efficiency, reduces data duplication, and enhances the ability to locate burial sites. The study concludes that implementing a web-based system significantly enhances municipal cemetery management. INTRODUCTION Background of the Study The Municipal Civic Registrar Office of LGU Carmen currently handles a large amount of information regarding burial permits and cemetery records. However, the office still relies on a manual process and basic Excel spreadsheets to manage this data. Because of these records are stored in separate, unlinked files, it becomes challenging for staff to maintain data integrity, frequently leading to record duplication. Furthermore, the lack of centralized, visual system makes it extremely difficult to find the specific location of a corpse or identify available burial plots. This project proposes a web-based system that integrates permit processing with geomapping technology to replace these disorganized tracking methods. Objectives of the Study The primary objective of this study is to develop a web-based burial permit processing and geomapped cemetery information system for the Municipal Civic Registrar Office of LGU Carmen.. The system is designed to move the office away from manual paperwork and basic Excel files by providing a centralized digital platform. Key features include a structured SQL database to store the personal details of the deceased, a digital log for burial permits, and a geomapping interface that uses precise coordinates to help staff visually locate specific graves and monitor how much space is left in the cemetery. While the system uses geomapping for location tracking, it will not involve real-time physical monitoring of the cemetery grounds through hardware like sensors or cameras. Furthermore, the study focuses on the administrative side of permit processing and record-keeping; it does not include features for public payment transactions or the physical maintenance of the burial sites themselves. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK Review of Related Literature The modernization of municipal services through digital transformation is essential for efficient urban management, particularly in the administration of public cemeteries. As urban populations grow, traditional manual record-keeping and disconnected spreadsheets often fail to provide the data integrity and spatial accuracy required for effective operations. Research indicates that the adoption of a Cemetery Information System (CIS) can provide a planned decision-support framework by utilizing web-based technologies and geomapping to resolve issues such as duplicated permits and the inability to locate specific burial sites. Furthermore, the integration of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) allows for precise grave zoning and cemetery mapping, ensuring that local government units can maintain an accurate, all-accessible registration database. The following studies explore the factors influencing the transition from manual to automated cemetery management and the impact of technological tools on public service delivery. In the study conducted by Chibuye and Phiri (2022), the authors evaluated the transition from traditional to digital record-keeping at the Lusaka City Council using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). Their research highlighted that the current reliance on manual models and basic spreadsheets makes it increasingly difficult to update records, locate specific burial slots, and maintain accurate cemetery mapping. This lack of centralized data often leads to a "deplorable state" within cemeteries, where grieving relatives struggle to find the graves of their departed family members for maintenance. The authors argued that migrating to an electronic management systemâ€”such as a web portal or mobile applicationâ€”is essential to improve productivity, increase customer satisfaction, and provide the public with easier access to vital information. Ultimately, their findings supported the "perceived usefulness" of adopting a digital Cemetery Information System (CIS) to resolve the inefficiencies inherent in manual municipal service delivery. Bahaludin and Mardhiah (2025) developed the Perlis E-Cemetery Dashboard, a sustainable solution designed to address the challenges of limited cemetery land through efficient resource management. Their study focused on integrating demographic analytics, predictive modeling, and interactive visualization tools to forecast annual land utilization and estimate when full capacity will be reached. The results showed that the system improved proactive decision-making for stakeholders, facilitated sustainable land use in alignment with Islamic Cemetery Planning Guidelines, and provided a scalable model for nationwide urban planning. Mohamad et al. (2023) developed a digital model utilizing Geographic Information System (GIS) technology and the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) to identify suitable locations for new cemeteries. Their study emphasized a systematic approach to site selection based on multi-criteria spatial factors, including soil type, gradient, elevation, and proximity to water sources and residential areas. The results showed that the weighted overlay model increased the efficiency of urban planning by quickly generating potential locations that met official sustainable development guidelines while reducing the errors associated with manual surveying methods. Budiani et al. (2026) developed a mobile web-based water monitoring tool called "Pantir" integrated into multi-storied cemetery designs to address burial space shortages in limited urban land areas. Their study emphasized real-time, high-accuracy groundwater level monitoring using laser sensors and microcontrollers to determine the technical feasibility and capacity of multi-storied grave structures. The results showed that the system effectively provided data that influenced structural designâ€”allowing up to 4â€“6 bodies per plotâ€”thereby offering a viable spatial solution that adheres to environmental monitoring needs and regional regulations. Their study emphasized the integration of Global Positioning Systems (GPS) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) to map burial plots and manage records in a digital environment. Their study emphasized that the scarcity of amenitiesâ€”such as adequate burial space, proper maintenance, and surveillanceâ€”creates significant challenges for urban management systems. The results showed that inefficient methods of handling burial demands in city planning negatively impact citizens' quality of life, highlighting the urgent need for efficacy and sufficiency in management solutions to protect environmental health. Bora et al. (2025) developed a Smart Cemetery System (SCS) that applies a cognitive ergonomics approach to optimize the management of public burial grounds. Their study emphasized the use of technology to improve the interaction between cemetery managers and the physical environment, focusing on enhancing the usability and efficiency of administrative tasks. The results showed that integrating smart systems into cemetery operations reduces cognitive load for staff, improves the organization of burial data, and leads to more effective resource management within urban public facilities. Rahman et al. (2025) designed, developed, and implemented a digital burial record-keeping and management system in Bangladesh to improve the monitoring of mortality data in urban settings. Their study emphasized the use of a digital platform to replace traditional manual registration, ensuring the systematic collection of death information and geographic location data. The results showed that the system enhanced the accuracy and timeliness of burial reporting, provided a centralized database for public health surveillance, and streamlined the administrative workflow for city corporations and graveyard authorities. Their study emphasized the complexities of navigating local government regulations, instances of discrimination from clients, and concerns regarding environmental protection and health safety. The results showed that caretakers employ various resilience strategies to address inadequate wages and job safety concerns, while evaluating the effectiveness of government support mechanisms provided to assist them in their roles. OPERATIONAL FRAMEWORK Materials 1.2.1 1.6.1  Software The software backend framework for this project is built using Laravel 12 to develop a web based system for Burial Permit Processing and Geomapped Cemetery Information for Municipal Civic Registrar Office of LGU Carmen. The software frontend is developed using React.js for a very responsive interface and TailwindCSS for a modern and premium design that stands out from the basic web application look and feel. Data storage and management is handled using MySQL which is locally hosted within the XAMPP server environment and stores all burial related information in a normalised format including details of deceased persons, burial permits, details of the cemetery or cemeteries involved and user information. The system also enables the location of spaces of niche and graves. This will facilitate the location of space of niche and graves by viewing an interactive and meaningful cemetery map. Detailed lines of plans of niche will also be outlined and accurately overlaid or superimposed on the map of cemeteries in order to facilitate the identification of location of space of niche and graves easier and accurate. The system are developed by using open source technology that able to provide ease and efficient functionality. The system is built using Laravel 12 framework and React.js for building front end and MySQL database management system for storing and management of data and information. Open source MapLibre GL Open Source Mapping Library technology are used for compositing the map of cemeteries. The build of front end of system are using Vite build tool for speeding up the build process. The system that have been developed is robust, safe, scalable and can process burial permit, generate cemetery map, record and analytical report. 1.6.2 HARDWARE Hardware refers to the physical devices required to develop, test, and operate the web-based system. For system development, the researchers used a development laptop with at least an Intel Core i5 processor (or equivalent), 16GB RAM, and 512GB SSD storage. These specifications ensure smooth coding, testing, and system debugging. For deployment and actual use at the Municipal Civic Registrar Office, the system does not require high-end hardware since it is web-based. You can access the system using any device (computer desktop, laptop, tablet) with sufficient RAM (at least 4 GB, 8 GB or more recommended for map rendering), web browser (supporting hardware accelerated graphics) and internet connection. Interactions with MapLibre GL geomapping software are optimized with hardware acceleration support. Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge and similar browsers are supported. Internet connectivity is required for real-time data processing, system access, and synchronization of burial records and mapping data. 1.6.3 DATA Data refers to the structured information collected, processed, and stored in the system to support efficient cemetery management and burial permit processing. Agile was selected due to its iterative nature, allowing the development team to continuously adapt the system based on ongoing feedback from the Municipal Civic Registrar Office. The process was divided into short development sprints focusing on core modules: Database Normalization, Authentication & Roles, Geomapping Interface (GIS), Permit Processing, and the Data Quality Scanner. 3.2 System Architecture The system employs a modern Client-Server Architecture. The client-side (Frontend) is built using React.js and TailwindCSS, providing a dynamic Single Page Application (SPA) feel for the geomapping and dashboard components. The server-side (Backend) utilizes Laravel 12 (PHP) to process business logic, handle API requests from the frontend, and enforce role-based access control. Data is persistently stored in a normalized MySQL database. 3.3 Data Gathering Procedure To ensure the system accurately addresses the needs of LGU Carmen, data was gathered through direct interviews with the Municipal Civic Registrar staff. Existing manual records and Excel spreadsheets were collected, audited for duplication, and migrated into the new SQL database to serve as the initial master data for the system's launch. System Architecture The system is based on a modern Client-Server Architecture. On the client side (Frontend) the application is implemented as Single Page Application (SPA) based on the React.js library and the UI framework TailwindCSS for geomapping and dashboard components. On the server side (Backend) the business logic is processed by the Laravel 12  framework written in PHP. The Backend processes the API requests from frontend and applies a role-based access control.
